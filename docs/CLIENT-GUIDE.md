# Updating the website

You can change the website yourself, without needing anyone to do it for you.
You describe what you want in plain English, you get a link to look at, and when
you're happy you say so. Nothing goes live until you say it does.

You don't need to know anything technical. You won't see any code.

## Making a change

**1. Go to the requests page**

<https://github.com/Benicians-Adopting-Benicia/benicians-adopting-benicia.github.io/issues>

Click the green **New issue** button.

**2. Pick what kind of change you want**

You'll see a short list — changing some wording, adding an event, posting a
newsletter, adding photos, or "something else" if none of them fit. Pick the
closest one. If in doubt, use *Something else*.

**3. Fill in the boxes and click Submit**

Write it however feels natural. "The phone number in the footer is wrong, it
should be 707-555-1234" is perfect. You don't need to use special words.

To add a photo or a PDF, click inside the box and drag the file in. Wait for it
to finish uploading before you submit.

**4. Wait a few minutes**

You'll get a reply explaining what was changed, and then a **preview link**.

**5. Click the preview link and look at it**

This is a practice copy of the website with your change in it. The real website
hasn't changed yet — nobody else is seeing this.

**6. Say what you want to happen**

- **Happy with it?** Reply with the single word **publish**. Your change goes
  live on the real website a minute or two later.

- **Not quite right?** Just reply saying what you'd like different — "can the
  heading be bigger?" or "actually let's say 'volunteers' instead of 'members'".
  You'll get a new preview to look at. Go round as many times as you need.

## Things worth knowing

**Nothing goes live until you say "publish".** You can leave a request sitting
for a week, change your mind, or ignore it entirely. Nothing happens on its own.

**Say just the word "publish" on its own.** If you write "can you publish this?"
it'll be read as another change request rather than as a go-ahead.

**Nothing is ever really lost.** Every change is saved. If something goes live
and you don't like it, open a new request using *Undo a recent change* and
describe what you want put back.

**Send the biggest photos you have.** They'll be shrunk to the right size
automatically. A photo straight off your phone is ideal.

**Ask for the outcome, not the method.** "The Donate button is hard to find on
my phone" usually gets a better result than "make the Donate button 20% bigger",
because it leaves room to find the best fix.

**If something looks broken or the site is down**, don't file a request — call
or text Matt.

## What if it gets it wrong?

Tell it. Reply on the same request saying what's off, and you'll get a new
preview. Nothing you say on a request can break the website — the worst that can
happen is a preview that isn't right, and previews aren't public.

If a request gets stuck or the reply doesn't make sense, Matt can step in. The
whole history of every request stays on the page, so there's always a record of
what was asked for.
